# Two-host script style (Anna and Ben)

Written after the livelier Brussels pilot and the listener's feedback: "much better, but room to improve" (more humor and banter, better voices).
Every two-host episode follows this. The facts stay exactly as accurate as before; only the telling changes.

## The hosts
- **Anna** is curious and quick. She asks the blunt question, makes the jokes, bets on what happens next and is often wrong, and sums up a messy century in one flat sentence.
- **Ben** has read everything. He is dry, deadpan, loves a tangent, and corrects her with "Not quite." He is sometimes caught out himself, and says so.
- They like each other. The teasing is warm. They never talk down to the listener.

## Banter
- A real laugh line about every 45 to 60 seconds, not every turn. The humor comes from the material: irony, absurd facts, a ridiculous coincidence. No forced puns.
- Running gags per episode, with callbacks (Anna's bets; a "stubbornness tally"; Ben's "we'll get there"). A gag lands twice or three times, never more.
- Friendly disagreement and being wrong are good. Anna guesses, Ben deflates, Anna recovers.
- Mock outrage, understatement and deadpan work well. Sarcasm about people is out.
- When the subject is a tragedy (war, victims, atrocities), drop the jokes completely and talk plainly. Humor never comes at the expense of accuracy or of victims.

## Turns
- Most turns are one to three sentences, under 45 words. Many are one to four words: "Right." "Wait, what?" "Ha."
- Never more than two long turns in a row from one host. Break an explanation up with a question, a reaction, or a guess.
- Spoken language: contractions, fragments, "Okay." "Hm." Thinking aloud and half-sentences are fine.
- Start each chapter with a hook (a question, a bet, a surprising fact), not a summary. End with a short teaser for the next one.

## For the voices
- Years as numerals, except three-digit years, which are spelled out ("nine seventy-seven").
- No parentheses, no stage directions, no long lists of names. Hard names go in tools/pronunciations.json.
- Ellipses and dashes sparingly: they become pauses.
- Short punchlines get their own turn, so the voice can leave a beat before them.
