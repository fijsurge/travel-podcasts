## Notes before you listen (skip this part when listening)

A short walk with two stops, designed for Saturday, December 5, after a short cab ride from Mr. Beef, about seven minutes north. The route is a little over half a mile, on residential streets, and it is easy in winter if the sidewalks are clear. Stop positions are approximate, so keep a map open. This is a neighborhood of private homes, so look and listen from the sidewalk.

## Welcome

Anna: Hello, and welcome to the last walk. I'm Anna.

Ben: And I'm Ben. After beef, you take a short cab ride north to Old Town, for two stops, and an afternoon drink.

Anna: And it's a winter stroll.

Ben: It is. Bundle up.

Anna: Bet. The houses will all look like gingerbread.

Ben: Some of them, honestly, do.

## Stop 1: Menomonee Street

Anna: Okay, we're out of the cab. It's quiet, and the houses are small.

Ben: You're on Menomonee Street, in the part of Old Town called the Old Town Triangle. Look at the houses. Some are small wooden cottages, and some are brick.

Anna: Why is it so... cottagey?

Ben: Because of a fire. German immigrants settled this area in the 1850s, and community life centered on Saint Michael Church. Then came the Great Chicago Fire of 1871.

Anna: The big one.

Ben: It destroyed all the houses here and most of the church. This is a serious part of the story, and it's why the street looks like it does. Residents rebuilt quickly with small frame worker's-style cottages.

Anna: Wood, after a fire?

Ben: Yes, and then a city ordinance banned further wood construction. Later houses were built of brick and stone, and that's the mix you see. The district includes at least one fire relief cottage.

Anna: A relief cottage. So these are houses made by charity.

Ben: The district notes at least one. Look for a very small, simple wooden house, and imagine how many people needed one.

Anna: Okay. I'm looking at it differently now.

Ben: Now look at the brick garages with apartments above. Those were built for the chauffeurs of nearby Gold Coast residents.

Anna: The chauffeurs lived over the garage. Ha. A tiny apartment above a car.

Ben: And the neighborhood almost didn't last. In the 1940s, community concern led to what's described as one of the nation's earliest neighborhood revitalization efforts, which helped keep these narrow, tree-lined streets and their character.

Anna: So some of the people who live here fought to save it.

Ben: They did. Walk slowly. When you're ready, head west toward Sedgwick Street, and the tavern is on the corner.

## Stop 2: Twin Anchors

Anna: There's an anchor on the sign. And a lot of brick.

Ben: This is Twin Anchors, at 1655 North Sedgwick Street. It's been a tavern since 1932, in a three-story brick building from 1881 that was once a brewery.

Anna: 1932. That's Prohibition.

Ben: Yes. It began as a place called Tante Lee Soft Drinks, where "soft drinks" was a code for alcohol. One account says a secret door in the south wall let people escape if the police raided the place.

Anna: A secret door. I'm putting that in the bet column.

Ben: It's the restaurant's own story, so enjoy it as that.

Anna: And Sinatra?

Ben: Frank Sinatra was a regular in the 1950s and 1960s. He had a favorite booth, and a phone jack was installed so he could take calls there.

Anna: A phone jack. In the booth. Of course.

Ben: And in 1981, when he headlined ChicagoFest, he ordered a fifty-rib dinner for himself and his staff.

Anna: Fifty ribs.

Ben: The restaurant is known for its ribs. They're cooked twice a day, every day, and it serves an estimated twelve hundred racks a week.

Anna: Twelve hundred. Okay, I'm hungry again.

Ben: You can ask about the Sinatra booth when you go in. The tavern also appeared in two films, Return to Me in 1999 and The Dark Knight in 2008.

Anna: And who owns it now?

Ben: Phillip and Laureen Tuzi bought it in 1978. Their children run it today, so it's the third family to own it.

Anna: A third family. In a place that old. That's pretty good.

Ben: That's a good ending for the afternoon. Order a drink, and pause here.

## Wrap-up

Anna: So that's Old Town. A fire, a rebuild, a booth, and a rib.

Ben: And a secret door, though I'd call that a legend.

Anna: I'd call it a perfect story.

Ben: Both. From here, it's a twelve-minute rideshare south, about two and a third miles, back to the hotel. You'll have your late checkout at four, then the airport.

Anna: Safe travels.

Ben: Safe travels.
